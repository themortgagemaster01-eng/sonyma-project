Clarity

Recapture tax sounds scary.

Let’s break it down.

First, don’t panic. Federal recapture tax does not automatically apply simply because you use a SONYMA mortgage.

The 3 things that must all happen

All three conditions below must be true before any potential recapture tax analysis even begins.

1

You sell or otherwise dispose of the home

The sale or disposition generally must occur within the first nine years after you buy the home with the SONYMA loan.

2

You make a net profit

The sale must result in a gain under the applicable federal recapture rules. If you sell at a loss or break even, this condition is not met.

3

Your household income exceeds the IRS threshold

At the time of sale, your income must exceed the applicable federal threshold for that year. Many households stay below the threshold.

ALL 3 MUST HAPPEN
1 + 2 + 3 = Potential Recapture Tax
If any one condition is missing, the special federal recapture tax generally does not apply.

If the sale occurs within nine years, produces a gain, and household income at the time of sale exceeds the applicable IRS threshold, then a potential recapture situation may exist. The actual tax (if any) is calculated under federal rules and can be limited.

This is not tax advice. The actual calculation depends on the applicable federal rules and individual circumstances. Consult a qualified tax professional for advice specific to your situation.

What does this mean for you?

Simply having a SONYMA mortgage does not mean you will owe recapture tax. Many borrowers never meet all three conditions. Understanding the rules helps you plan, but the decision to use SONYMA should be based on the overall fit of rates, down-payment options, and program features for your purchase.
