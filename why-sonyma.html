Why are buyers choosing SONYMA?

SONYMA offers 30-year fixed mortgages with competitive rates, down payment assistance, longer lock periods, and flexible property options for qualified New York buyers.

01

Lower than average rates

Both Achieving the Dream and the Low Interest Rate Program are 30-year fixed mortgages designed to offer competitive pricing below typical conventional rates for eligible buyers.

02

Lower down payment

Down payment as low as 3% of the purchase price. Minimum borrower cash contribution is typically 1% (3% for co-ops). Financing up to 97% available for many qualified 1-unit purchases.

03

Down payment assistance (DPAL)

Eligible borrowers may use SONYMA’s DPAL — 0% interest, no monthly payment, generally the greater of $3,000 or 3% of the purchase price (up to $15,000). Forgiven over 10 years if you stay in the home.

04

Longer lock periods

120-day interest rate locks for existing housing (with extension options commonly available). Longer 240-day locks available for new construction, rehabilitation, cooperatives, or distressed sales.

05

Flexible property types

Eligible property types include single-family homes, condominiums, cooperatives (co-ops), and 2–4 family homes used as a primary residence (subject to program guidelines and age/occupancy rules for multi-family).

06

Target Area benefits

Buying in a designated Target Area may allow a waiver of the first-time homebuyer requirement, plus higher income and purchase-price limits in many counties.

At a glance

What both main programs share

  • 30-year fixed-rate mortgage only
  • No points
  • Down payment as low as 3% + optional DPAL assistance
  • 120-day rate lock on existing homes (extensions available through many lenders)
  • Available for single-family, condo, co-op, and 2–4 family primary residences
  • No prepayment penalties
  • Can be combined with other grants and subsidies in many cases

Achieving the Dream is geared toward lower-income households and carries the lowest rates. The Low Interest Rate Program serves a broader moderate-income range with slightly higher rates.
