SONYMA questions, answered

Short, clear answers to the questions first-time buyers and realtors ask most often.

Many SONYMA programs allow a down payment as low as 3% of the purchase price. Minimum borrower cash contribution is often 1% (3% for co-ops). You may also pair the loan with DPAL to help cover down payment and eligible closing costs, subject to guidelines.
Not always. Many SONYMA programs are designed for first-time buyers, but exceptions exist for purchases in designated Target Areas and certain other situations under program guidelines. The best way to know is to review your specific county, property, and household situation.
If you have owned a home in the past three years, you generally do not meet the standard first-time buyer definition. However, buying in a SONYMA Target Area can change that analysis. Always verify with current program rules and a lender.
A Target Area is a federally designated geographic area where SONYMA can offer more flexible first-time buyer rules and, in many cases, higher income and purchase-price limits. Look up any address with the official tool: SONYMA Target Area Locator.
Yes, in many cases. Single-family homes, condos, co-ops, and 2–4 family homes used as a primary residence can be eligible depending on the program, occupancy rules, and underwriting requirements. Purchase-price limits and minimum cash contribution can vary by property type.
DPAL is SONYMA’s Down Payment Assistance Loan. Standard DPAL is generally a 0% interest loan with no monthly payment that can help cover down payment and eligible closing costs. The amount is typically the greater of $3,000 or 3% of the purchase price, up to $15,000, subject to current program terms. Using DPAL usually increases the first-mortgage rate by a set amount (commonly 0.40%).
DPAL PLUS 2026 is an enhanced, limited down-payment assistance option for certain lower-income buyers. Under current guidelines it can provide more assistance than standard DPAL (in some cases up to higher amounts) for down payment, closing costs, and related costs. Funding is limited and eligibility rules are stricter. Confirm current availability with Robert or the official SONYMA materials.
Yes, if you meet the requirements of the specific SONYMA program and the assistance product (such as standard DPAL or limited DPAL PLUS offerings). Assistance is subject to funding availability, income limits, and other guidelines.
It depends on the program and your situation. Many SONYMA borrowers are required to complete an approved homebuyer education course before closing. The course is designed to help you understand credit, budgeting, mortgage terms, and the responsibilities of homeownership. Requirements can vary—ask your lender what applies to your file.
Federal recapture tax is a potential tax that can apply in limited situations when you sell a home financed with certain mortgage revenue bond programs (including many SONYMA loans) within nine years and meet specific income and gain conditions. It does not automatically apply just because you used SONYMA. See the Recapture Tax page for a plain-English breakdown.
Yes. You can sell the home. Whether any federal recapture tax applies depends on the timing of the sale, whether you have a gain, and your income at the time of sale relative to IRS thresholds. Most borrowers never owe recapture tax.
Refinancing is possible in many situations, but SONYMA has specific rules about when and how a SONYMA loan can be refinanced. Talk with your lender about current options and any restrictions that may apply to your loan.
